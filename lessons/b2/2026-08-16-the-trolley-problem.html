The Trolley Problem: Would You Pull the Lever?

一道没有标准答案的伦理难题 ｜ 全文阅读（点高亮词看中文释义，点 ▶ 听朗读）

1
Paragraph 1 · Hook

Imagine a runaway trolley is speeding toward five workers trapped on the track. You stand beside a lever. If you pull it, the trolley will switch to a side track — where one person stands. One dies; five live. This is the trolley problem, a famous hypothetical dilemma in moral philosophy first posed by Philippa Foot in 1967.

2
Paragraph 2 · The twist

Most people say they would pull the lever. Yet the puzzle deepens with a twist. Suppose you stand on a footbridge above the track. There is no lever, but a large stranger stands beside you. Would you push him off to stop the trolley, sacrificing one to save five? Although the numbers are identical, most people refuse. Why does the same consequence feel so different?

3
Paragraph 3 · Why it matters

Philosophers use this gap to test rival theories. A utilitarian argues we should maximise total welfare, so saving five beats saving one. But our moral intuition often rejects pushing the stranger, because doing so is a direct act of killing. Foot claimed we can distinguish between killing and merely letting someone die. The rationale is that intending harm is impermissible, even for a greater good.

4
Paragraph 4 · Reflection

The trolley problem has no single answer. Instead, it reveals how we weigh lives, intentions, and rules. Today it shapes real debates — from self-driving cars to wartime decisions. moral reasoning itself became clearer only because philosophers kept asking this odd question.

💡 句型练习 Sentence Patterns (B2 Advanced)

1. Had [subject] + past participle, [main clause with would/might/could].

过去虚拟语气倒装：省略 if，把 had 提到主语前，相当于 "If philosophers had never asked..."，表示与过去事实相反的假设。
Had philosophers never asked this odd question, we might understand our moral reasoning far less clearly.
练习：用 "she read the case / she would not see the flaw" → , she would not see the flaw.

2. [Gerund phrase] + is + adjective / noun phrase.

动名词（非谓语动词）短语作主语，表示一种行为或事实；谓语用单数。Sacrificing... 整体充当主语。
Sacrificing one to save five is, for many people, impossible to justify.
练习：用 "pushing the stranger / feels wrong" → feels deeply wrong to most of us.

3. Although [clause], [contrasting clause].

让步状语从句：Although 引导从句，主句用转折语义，强调"即使事实如此，结果却相反"，是学术写作常用衔接。
Although the numbers are identical, most people refuse to push the stranger.
练习：用 "the cost is lower / few accept the trade-off" → , few accept the trade-off.

4. A utilitarian argues (that) [noun clause].

动词 + (that) 引导的名词性从句，作 argue / claim / suggest 的宾语，用于转述他人观点，是议论文的核心句式。
A utilitarian argues (that) we should maximise total welfare.
练习：用 "Foot claimed / we can distinguish two acts" → we can distinguish killing from letting die.

5. Instead, [restatement or contrast].

衔接副词：Instead / Yet / Moreover 用于引出转折或递进，使语篇逻辑清晰、富于层次，是 B2 高级写作的标志。
The trolley problem has no single answer. Instead, it reveals how we weigh lives, intentions, and rules.
练习：用 "the rule seems simple / it hides a deep conflict" → The rule seems simple; , it hides a deep conflict.

📐 语法点 Grammar

1. 虚拟语气倒装（Inverted subjunctive）

当 if 条件句含 had / were / should 时，可省略 if 并把动词提到主语前，语气更正式、书面，是 B2 高级写法。
Had philosophers never asked this odd question, we might understand our reasoning far less clearly.

2. 非谓语动词 · 动名词作主语（Gerund as subject）

动词加 -ing 可作名词用，放在句首充当主语，表示一种活动或事实，谓语用单数。
Sacrificing one to save five is impossible to justify for many. / Pushing the stranger feels wrong.

3. 让步状语从句（Concessive clause）

用 Although / Though / Even if 引导，主句表达与从句预期相反的结果，突出对比，常见于议论文。
Although the numbers are identical, most people refuse to push the stranger.

4. 名词性从句（Noun clause after a verb）

argue / claim / suggest 后接 (that) 从句，整个从句充当宾语，用来客观转述观点，避免武断。
A utilitarian argues (that) we should maximise total welfare. / Foot claimed we can distinguish two acts.

5. 语篇衔接词（Linking adverbs）

Yet（然而）、Instead（反而）、Although（尽管）等衔接副词让句与句之间的转折、递进关系清晰，是学术写作连贯性的关键。
Most people would pull the lever. Yet the puzzle deepens with a twist. / Instead, it reveals how we weigh lives.

✅ 练习测验 Quiz

🌏 中文知识扩展

一句话讲清"电车难题"

一辆失控的电车（trolley）正冲向铁轨上的五个人。你手边有一个拉杆：拉下它，电车会转向侧轨，但侧轨上站着一个人。于是你面临选择——不拉，死五个；拉，死一个、救五个。这就是"电车难题"，由英国哲学家菲利帕·福特（Philippa Foot）在 1967 年提出，后经朱迪思·贾维斯·汤姆森（Judith Jarvis Thomson）在 1976 年系统化并命名。它不是真要你当司机，而是一个思想实验（thought experiment）：用来探测我们道德直觉背后的逻辑。

为什么"拉杆"和"推人"感觉不一样？

最经典的变体是天桥版本（footbridge）：这次没有拉杆，你站在一座天桥上，身边有个大块头陌生人。如果把他推下去，他的身体能挡停电车、救下五人。结果同样是"死一救五"，但绝大多数人会拒绝推人。同样的数字，为什么观感天差地别？哲学家提出两种解释：

  • 杀人与容许死亡（killing vs. letting die）：福特认为，拉杆是"让一个人死"，而推人是"主动杀死一个人"，二者道德分量不同。
  • 双重效应原则（Doctrine of Double Effect）：为了更大善而间接造成的伤害可被接受，但直接把人当工具去杀，则不可接受。

两种大理论的正面交锋

电车难题几乎是功利主义（utilitarianism）与义务论（deontology）的缩影：

  • 功利主义（以边沁、密尔、彼得·辛格为代表）：看"结果总量"。五条命大于一条命，所以拉杆甚至推人都"应该"。它追求最大化总体福祉。
  • 义务论（以康德为代表）：某些行为本身就是错的（如谋杀），不能因为结果好就正当化。把陌生人当"挡车工具"违背了对人的尊重，无论救多少人都不允许。

神经科学也来凑热闹：脑成像显示，拉杆激活的是理性脑区（算人数），而推人激活的是情绪脑区（对"亲手杀人"的厌恶）。所以我们的选择，可能一半是逻辑、一半是本能。

它不只是"脑洞"：现实中的影子

电车难题早已走出哲学课堂，进入真实决策：

  • 自动驾驶（self-driving cars）：当碰撞不可避免时，算法该优先保护车内乘客还是路人？这本质是"编程版电车难题"。
  • 战伦理与无人机：空袭中"附带伤亡（collateral damage）"能否被接受，对应"间接伤害 vs. 直接杀害"的区分。
  • 医疗资源分配：疫情中有限的呼吸机给谁用，也是"一换多"的功利计算。

学习者的收获

电车难题没有标准答案，这正是它的价值：它不教你对错，而是教你如何为自己的判断辩护。当你能说清"我选拉杆，因为……""我拒绝推人，因为……"，你就已经在做真正的哲学思考了。这也提醒我们：在 B2 写作与口语中，用 Although / Instead / A utilitarian argues that… 这类句式，能让你把"我觉得"变成"我论证"。
